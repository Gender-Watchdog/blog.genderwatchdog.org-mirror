*October 4, 2026 — Gender Watchdog*

**On September 20, 2026, TIFF posted a celebratory premiere caption for a Korean film and named its cast. On October 2, that film's entire cast and crew cancelled their appearances at BIFF's opening ceremony, citing safety. TIFF said nothing between those dates, and has said nothing since. This post is not about that film. It is about the pipeline behind the films these festivals celebrate — and that pipeline runs through Dongguk University. The record of what Dongguk University has done, and not done, is now documented thoroughly enough to present in full.**

---

## The Pipeline

BIFF 2026 opens October 6. Three days later — October 9 — Canada's statutory deadline expires for file A-2026-00787: Gender Watchdog's access-to-information request asking whether Canada's own government treats GW as a human rights defender under its Voices at Risk program. Between those two dates, this post lays out what the pipeline behind the festival has done with institutional accountability.

Dongguk University is Korea's oldest Buddhist institution, founded 1906. Its stated mission includes truthfulness (진리), compassion (자비), and righteousness (정직). It houses one of Korea's most prominent film and digital media graduate schools — a direct feeder into the industry BIFF and TIFF celebrate.

The documented record of that institution follows.

---

## Pillar I: Faculty Made Unreachable

On April 1, 2026 — eight days after the EU-Korea Research and Innovation Day at the Four Seasons Hotel Seoul — Visual Ping's automated monitoring flagged a set of coordinated changes to Dongguk's Graduate School of Digital Image & Contents faculty page.[^1]

Kim Jongwan (김종완), a senior film directing professor with a doctorate in film media production from Dongguk's own graduate program, was simultaneously given the title 명예교수 (emeritus/honorary professor) and stripped of every institutional contact field: both phone numbers, the 교수연구실 label, his office room (문화관 4층 K413). Nothing was added. Only a personal Naver email — not an institutional address — remained.[^1]

명예교수 is the honorific awarded on retirement from active professorship in Korean universities. It is prestigious, visible on the faculty roster, and counted in accreditation headcounts. It carries no salary, no continuing supervisory authority over enrolled students, and no obligation to respond through official Dongguk channels. The mechanism is precise: ceremonial elevation, functional erasure. Kim Jongwan's name and credentials remain on the public roster. His institutional reachability does not.

Visual Ping caught the exact moment Kim Jongwan's title changed and his contact disappeared — the diff shows it in red.

By September 9, 2026, a prospective student posted on DC Inside's Dongguk University board: "I noticed 3 film department professors have been changed to emeritus on the homepage — if I enroll next year, will they all have been replaced?"[^1] 69 views. Zero responses.

The same contact-stripping pattern had appeared at Dongguk's **undergraduate** film department (예술대학 영화영상학과, movie.dongguk.edu) — not just the grad school. Jeong Jaehyeong (정재형), a film studies specialist with a PhD from Chung-Ang University, is listed 명예교수 with 연락처 (phone): `–` and 교수연구실 (office): `–`.[^18]

The contrast within the same page makes the pattern precise: of the three undergrad emeriti, only 정재형 has contact stripped. 유지나 (Yoo Jina — a prominent feminist film critic, Paris 7 PhD) retains full phone and office. 문원립 retains full phone and office. Contact-stripping is not a feature of emeritus status at Dongguk. It is specific to directing-specialised male faculty.

The sharpest detail is on 정재형's own profile page:[^19]

He is still assigned courses. He cannot be reached through any official Dongguk contact channel. Students enrolled in his classes have no institutional pathway to contact the person teaching them.

Two departments. Two directing-specialised male professors. Same mechanism — ceremonial elevation, functional erasure. For Kim Jongwan, the timing is documented: the Horizon Europe GEP compliance audit was active from March 24, 2026, and his contact was stripped eight days later.[^1] The same contact-stripping pattern at the undergraduate department was documented by September 9, 2026 — observed by a prospective student nine days before BIFF opened.

**The graduate school's tenured and tenure-track faculty tier (교수/조교수) is 100% male in both July 2025 and April 2026 snapshots.**[^1]

Against this composition: **61.5% of female Korean university arts and culture students experience sexual violence**, predominantly from the male-dominated faculty structure, according to the Korean Women's Development Institute's 2020 research.[^2] Gender Watchdog's structural analysis of that same KWDI data assigns film and media programs the highest risk score: **81 out of 100** — attributable to GW's analysis, not to the KWDI report itself.[^2] That analysis is the baseline for why contact-stripping in a film school's directing faculty is not an administrative formality.

GW's May 2025 Xiaohongshu post on Dongguk's sexual violence crisis drew first-person testimonies from students who had direct experiences at the institution — including an account of a graduate admissions panellist asking questions "not about the interview — about my personal privacy."[^15] The post's content reached 3,421 views across 22 Korean university discussion boards through organic cross-platform spread.[^1]

## Pillar II: The Convicted Executive Who Became Dean

The individual on the English-language Dongguk faculty page listed as "Sung-Jai Tcha" with a credential of "Bachelor Degree / French Education" is Tcha Seung-jai (차승재).[^3]

The credential listing is inconsistent with his actual institutional role and with his listed specializations in film production. What is consistent with the public record:[^3] [^4]

- **March 2007:** Installed as the 4th Dean of Dongguk's Graduate School of Digital Image & Contents while simultaneously serving as co-CEO of Sidus FNH (one of Korea's five largest film production companies) and Chairman of the Korea Film Producers Association.
- **2015:** Indicted as an active Dongguk professor for bid-rigging of public education subsidies — funds misappropriated from a government film industry workforce-education scheme totalling approximately 350 million KRW (~CAD $340,000).
- **2017:** Convicted of bid-rigging (공개경쟁 입찰 방해) by Seoul Western District Court. Sentence: a fine of **1.5 million KRW (~$1,100 USD)**.[^3] He continued as a professor.
- **March 2020:** Appointed Director of the Image Culture & Contents Research Institute — 36 months post-conviction. Korean media coverage of the appointment mentioned no prior conviction.[^3]
- **March 2, 2023:** Installed as the **11th Dean** of the Graduate School — six years post-conviction, the second time holding the title. Confirmed by Yonhap News Agency. Dongguk's own institutional history page records it verbatim: `2023.03 · 제11대 원장에 영화영상제작학과 차승재 교수 취임`.[^3]

As of September 2025, Tcha was removed from the Korean-language faculty page. He remained on the English-language page as of April 2026 — listed with the French Education bachelor's degree, not his film production credentials, and still appearing at timestamp 1:28 in Dongguk's official graduate school promotional video on YouTube.[^4]

His institutional position during his first deanship created what GW's analysis describes as a **quadruple dependency relationship**: corporate control (co-CEO of Sidus FNH, co-located in the same building as the film school); academic authority (faculty member with direct control over grades and academic progression); industry gatekeeping (access to internships, casting, and professional connections); and industry association leadership (chairman of the Korea Film Producers Association, controlling industry-wide standards and networking).[^4] Every career pathway available to a Dongguk film student passed through, or was affected by, one individual. The conviction interrupted none of it.

## Pillar III: The Silencing Attempt

On May 27, 2025, Law Firm Shinwon sent Gender Watchdog a letter on behalf of Sidus FNH, demanding retraction of GW's documentation, a public apology, and threatening criminal and civil action under Korean law. Sidus's stated grounds: the company "has had no connection to Dongguk University for over 15 years."[^5]

Dongguk University's own English-language faculty page — archived April 8, 2025, still live as of Wayback Machine capture dated April 2, 2026 — carried the following statement addressed to its international audience:

**"The department is located in the building, so-called 'Choong-Moo-Ro Yeong-Sang Center,' in which Sidus FNH, one of the top five film production companies in Korea, is based."**[^4] [^5]

The denial, issued by Sidus's lawyers, is directly refuted by the institution Sidus shares space with. The legal threat meant to erase GW's documentation of the co-location was sent while Dongguk's own website continued to publish that co-location to any international visitor who looked. As of ten months after Sidus's letter, Dongguk's English page still carried the announcement.

GW filed complaints with bar associations in Korea, Asia, and internationally against Law Firm Shinwon and the attorney named in the correspondence. The threat is now part of the public record it sought to suppress.

## Pillar IV: Falsified Partnerships

Dongguk's international partnerships page listed universities with which it had no verified active agreement. Gender Watchdog's documentation identified **33+ falsified partnerships in total**.[^6]

On January 19, 2026, Visual Ping's automated monitoring caught Dongguk's website making two simultaneous changes in the same update: the University of British Columbia was deleted from the Global Partners page; Toronto Metropolitan University was simultaneously reverted to its pre-2022 name "Ryerson University" — a name the institution formally retired in April 2022.[^7] The revert is self-evidencing: a partner list that uses a name the institution abandoned four years earlier was not built from current communications.

The BC Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner oversaw a Freedom of Information search at UBC. It returned zero records of any communication with Dongguk or any UBC document mentioning "Dongguk" — absence consistent with no active agreement.[^7]

A second Canadian university denied any partnership with Dongguk in writing and asked GW not to name that institution to journalists or media. GW honours that request.[^7]

Dongguk has not responded to GW's documented outreach since April 10, 2025, when GW notified the university about the second Canadian institution's denial.[^6]

## Pillar V: Canada's Response and Non-Response

End Rape on Campus (EROC) supported GW's documentation of sexual violence in Korean universities.[^8]

On June 17, 2025, the Canadian Trade Office in Taipei (CTOT) received GW's inquiry regarding Korean higher education safety concerns and directed GW to the Embassy of Canada in Seoul, describing the matter as a "sensitive matter."[^8]

On December 17, 2025, the Canadian Media Producers Association signed a Memorandum of Understanding with the Producers Guild of Korea — "promote international co-production opportunities," as CMPA described it. The MOU contains no mention of sexual violence rates among Korean film students or workers and no due-diligence framework for the institutions whose graduates feed CMPA member productions.[^9] GW's documentation of zero foreign women in Korean entertainment leadership positions — director, screenwriter, producer, executive — had been published six months before CMPA signed the MOU.[^10]

On September 9, 2026, GW filed an access-to-information request with Global Affairs Canada, acknowledged under **file A-2026-00787** on September 17, 2026.[^8] The 19-item request asks, among other questions, whether GW is treated as a human rights defender under Canada's Voices at Risk program, and whether any records at Canada's Seoul Embassy or CTOT reference GW's documentation of sexual violence cover-ups and falsified partnerships.

**The statutory 30-day response deadline is October 9, 2026 — three days after BIFF opens.** As of October 4, 2026, no response has been received.[^8]

## Pillar VI: The Guild That Asks for Protection But Gave None

On October 2, 2026, the Producers Guild of Korea issued a public statement regarding the controversy surrounding *The Assassin(s)*:

**"While historical events invite diverse interpretations, the current controversy has escalated beyond criticism of the work itself into direct threats against the production company, director, cast and crew."**[^11]

And: **"Exposing the personal details and family relationships of individuals who participated in a project, or exerting pressure on their normal activities, goes far beyond legitimate criticism and can never be justified."**[^11]

This is a legitimate ask. Physical threats against filmmakers are serious, and Gender Watchdog endorses the principle PGK articulated.

What PGK's December 17, 2025 Memorandum of Understanding with CMPA contains: no mention of the 61.5% sexual violence rate that female arts students face inside the Korean university pipeline PGK's members govern. No mention of the structural risk scores GW has documented. No mention of zero foreign women in any leadership position in the industry the MOU was designed to deepen co-production with. The MOU was framed entirely around content opportunity and industry partnership.[^9]

PGK's CMPA press release quote: "where the driving force of Korean cinema comes together... uniting the professionals who bring Korean films to life."[^9]

The argument here is not that physical threats and institutional sexual violence are equivalent harms — they are not. The argument is structural: "protection from threats" as a value invoked publicly in October 2026 was not applied to the 61.5% of female students in the pipeline PGK governs when PGK had the opportunity to write it into an institutional document nine months earlier. The absence is documented. The opportunity was concrete. The same institutions. The standard was not applied consistently.

## Pillar VII: What TIFF Didn't Stay Around to See

TIFF's two September posts named the cast and director of *The Assassin(s)* in pure-glamour framing. No mention of the film's subject — a real 1974 assassination attempt and a real first lady's death, fictionalized to imply government-official involvement. Nine days after TIFF's last post: a police defamation complaint filed against the director and producers.[^12] Twelve days after TIFF's last post: the cast withdrew from BIFF's opening ceremony, citing safety. The Cinematographers Guild of Korea: **"If discussions about a movie go too far and attack individual creators and actors, it could discourage filmmakers from trying new stories in the future."**[^11] As of October 4, 2026, TIFF has issued no statement.[^13]

This is the third documented instance of the same gap at the same festival in one cycle. GW's post "The Minister Who Was There" documents the first (Lee Chang-dong's Ebert Award and his 2003–2004 ministerial tenure).[^14] GW's sibling post on *The Assassin(s)* documents all three. The pattern is consistent across three different directors, three different specific conditions, one festival.

The film industry on display at BIFF and TIFF was built through a pipeline this post has now documented in full. The festivals that caption the product have shown no engagement with the pipeline that produced it.

## The Record Is Now Complete

Every step is documented, in sequence, with published primary sources:

- Faculty made institutionally unreachable through a mechanism that preserves accreditation value while eliminating every official contact pathway — as BIFF's opening week begins
- A convicted executive elevated twice to Dean, the second time six years after a public conviction, with a sentence of approximately $1,100 for bid-rigging $340,000 in public education funds
- A legal threat sent to the advocates who documented it — refuted by the institution it sought to protect, which continued publishing the co-location statement the threat demanded be retracted
- Partnerships falsified, caught mid-scrub, with the scrubbing method itself confirming that the list was not current
- Canada acknowledging the "sensitivity of the matter," then signing an MOU with no safety standards and allowing a statutory ATIP deadline to pass as BIFF opens
- Survivor testimony reaching 3,421 views across 22 university discussion boards without any institutional response from Dongguk — or from any Canadian body it lists as a partner

European research funding bodies are independently applying the institutional accountability questions that Canadian film bodies have not: the European Commission's Research and Technology Directorate confirmed to GW in April 2026 that GEP compliance review is active for every Korean institution in a Horizon Europe proposal.[^16]

**To PGK:** You asked on October 2, 2026, that the public refrain from threatening and defaming filmmakers. GW endorses that standard. Will you apply it to the 61.5% of female students in the Korean university arts pipeline whose sexual violence is documented by the KWDI — the pipeline your members govern? Your December 2025 MOU with CMPA contains no answer.

**To CMPA:** GW formally notified you of Dongguk's falsified international partnerships — including UBC — on February 18, 2026. You had signed the MOU with PGK two months earlier. What due-diligence framework, if any, applies to the institutions your members co-produce with?

**To TIFF:** Three documented gaps at your festival in one cycle. Has any internal process engaged the conditions behind the work you program?

**To GAC:** The statutory deadline for file A-2026-00787 is October 9. Canada's own Voices at Risk program exists to protect human rights defenders. GW's record — a Canadian-operated advocacy, a documented legal threat from a Korean law firm, a CTOT diplomatic acknowledgment of sensitivity — is documented. What is Canada's answer?

**To BIFF, Netflix, and Disney+:** The standing questions from GW's Post 1 in this series remain open: <a href="https://blog.genderwatchdog.org/biff-third-festival-nobody-asked/">https://blog.genderwatchdog.org/biff-third-festival-nobody-asked/</a>
